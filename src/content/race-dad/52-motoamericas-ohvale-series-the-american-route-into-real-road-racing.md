Canadian mini-racing families eyeing the next level eventually ask the same question: what does the American route look like? MotoAmerica's Ohvale classes are the answer south of the border — a real national series with a real ladder above it. Here is the map.

![A title card reading "The American ladder." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/motoamerica-ohvale-series/motoamerica-ohvale-series-01.png)

## The series

MotoAmerica is the United States' premier road racing championship, and its Ohvale classes are the junior rung of that ladder: mini road racers on equal machinery, racing at national events, in front of the organization that literally feeds American road racing's professional ranks. The classes mirror the bikes Canadian families already know — the Ohvale generations that fill MiniSBK and MotoMini grids — which is precisely why the crossing works: a kid who races Ohvales here is qualified on hardware, and only logistics stand between them and the grid.

The structure is the draw. Racing at MotoAmerica events means big-venue circuits, professional event standards, timing and presentation done the way the big classes do it, and paddocks where the next steps of the sport are visibly happening around the junior races. It is a kid-sized window into the full-size world, in a way club weekends, much as we love them, are not.

## How it connects to the ladder

The American route's real product is connection. MotoAmerica's junior and mini classes sit inside the same organization that runs the professional championship — the people running junior racing are the sport's pipeline people, and the riders who climb there are climbing in front of the teams who hire. For a Canadian family mapping a pathway — the conversation our road-to-MotoGP post opens — the US route is one of the two great corridors, and the Ohvale classes are its front door. Race well here and the reference travels; the American scene's results are legible everywhere.

## What Canadians should check first

Licences and paperwork before anything: cross-border racing means licence validity with your home federation and the event organizer, membership requirements, and the paperwork of minors racing internationally — none of it hard, all of it slower than you want the week of the event. Check the current rulebook and entry packages on motoamerica.com directly, because class structures, eligibility, and the calendar move year to year — this post is the map, not the timetable. Travel logistics are the other reality: circuits are a drive, bikes cross the border, and the season budget changes shape — our true-cost thinking applies with an exchange rate and a bigger map.

## The honest framing

The American route is worth it for the level, the venues, and the visibility — when the rider is ready for all three. It is not a shortcut; it is the same sport with a bigger audience and a longer drive. The families who get the most from it treat it as a deliberate season plan, not a splash trip: a target event or two, real preparation, and results read as data. The ladder is real, the door is genuinely open to Canadians, and the front door says Ohvale on it.
