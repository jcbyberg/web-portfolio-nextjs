Once a year, the minimoto world's center of gravity moves to Guangzhou — the Ohvale Guangzhou Supercup, the international event where the best mini racers on the planet trade the paddock they know for one that changes everything. For Canadian families, it matters more than the distance suggests.

![A title card reading "The world comes to Guangzhou." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/ohvale-guangzhou-supercup/ohvale-guangzhou-supercup-01.png)

## What it is

The Supercup is Ohvale's flagship international gathering: national-level riders from around the world, invited through their national scenes and selections, racing the full Ohvale classes at a level of density the club paddock never produces. Every fast kid from every country in one entry list. The machinery is equal, the preparation is not — the teams that arrive treat it like a world championship, because functionally it is one, and the racing is close in the way only genuinely deep grids can be.

## Who gets invited

The honest answer: the path runs through results, and the results that matter are the ones in your own national calendar first. Selection conversations — federation involvement, series performance, the standing of the rider in their class — start with what a kid does at home, in front of the people who fill in the forms. This is why the 190 class matters so much domestically: it is the shop window the selection looks through. There is no shortcut lane; there is being ready when the conversation happens, and our family lived the whole arc of getting there in the season we wrote about in the Ontario-to-Guangzhou posts.

## What the experience actually is

For a family, it is two things at once: a race meet and an expedition. The travel is serious — flights, bikes or arrangements, time zones, a week of your life — and the paddock is a world tour of mini racing: languages, team rigs, kids who are national heroes at twelve, and a standard of preparation that recalibrates everyone who sees it. The racing teaches things the club calendar cannot — different tracks, different conditions, rivals with completely different racing cultures — and the non-racing teaches more: how a kid carries themselves when the room is the whole world instead of the whole province.

## Why it matters on a resume

The Supercup line means something to the people who read junior racing resumes, because it means the rider was measured against the international field of their generation — whatever the finishing position says. Selections, teams, and programs up the ladder all know the event; a family that has been can also speak the language of the next level, which matters when those conversations get real. And beyond the racing economics, it is simply the trip: the kid who went to Guangzhou and lined up with the world is a different kid on the flight home. We would do it again in a heartbeat, and the posts from our trip are the evidence. The world comes to Guangzhou. Once you have seen it, you understand why the whole ladder leans toward it.
