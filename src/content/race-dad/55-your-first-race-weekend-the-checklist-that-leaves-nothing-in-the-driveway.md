The first race weekend is where the discovering happens: the gear you forgot, the tool you needed at 8am, the food situation at a track with one hot dog stand. This is the checklist we wish someone had handed us — organized by load zone, built to be copied.

![A title card reading "Nothing left in the driveway." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/first-race-weekend-checklist/first-race-weekend-checklist-01.png)

## Zone one: the machine

The bike, race-ready, checked against the pre-load checklist — oil fresh, chain set, bolts torqued, tire pressures at transport baseline. Fuel: race fuel or pump as your class requires, plus premix measured into a labeled jug if you are a two-stroke family (one jug, one bike — the 7am mixing disaster is real). Tools: the track-side kit — basic hand tools, tire gauge, plug kit and spare plugs, levers and a pre-bent spare, chain lube and a master link, zip ties, tape, fuses, cable ties' cousins in the hardware bag. Spares by probability: levers, bars if you have them, pegs, cables, tubes. The stand, the paddock mat, the fuel jug, the funnel.

## Zone two: the rider

Helmet, leathers, gloves — race gloves and a spare pair that is merely okay, back protector, boots, base layers, two sets minimum because race mornings do not negotiate with damp gear. Kid comfort: the second layer for cold mornings, the sunscreen, the water bottles, the snacks that survive a track day (granola bars, not just track fries). Personal: meds, glasses or contacts kit, the comfort item for between sessions. Tech inspection items: ensure helmet age and gear condition meet the rulebook — check your series' current requirements before packing, because tech rules are the one argument you cannot win at the booth.

## Zone three: the paddock

Canopy with weights (the canopy post is its own lecture), chairs, the folding table, the cooler. Paperwork: licence, series membership, entry confirmation, medical forms if your series requires them, parental consent docs — in a folder, in the vehicle, not in the email app with no signal. Power: charged everything the night before — transponder if you own one, radios if you use them, phones, cameras. Food plan for the whole family, because race food is a budget line and a nutrition line. Comfort: shade, layers for the weather you did not plan for, chairs for grandparents.

## Zone four: the invisible stuff

The schedule: know your check-in and tech inspection windows and first-session times before you arrive, and know where the schedule board lives at the venue. Cash for the gate and the burger stand. The folder with the rulebook link saved offline. A pen — someone always needs a pen. Duct tape. More water than that.

The checklist's job is brutal and simple: the weekend's memories should be about the racing, not the driving home for the thing in the driveway. Copy it, laminate it, tick it with a marker every load-out — and after the weekend, edit it, because your family's first race weekend is also the first draft of every packing list you will ever use again.
