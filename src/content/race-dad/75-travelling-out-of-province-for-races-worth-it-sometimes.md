Sooner or later the season's calendar raises the question: should we pack up and race somewhere else? Sometimes the answer is yes and it changes everything. Sometimes it is no, and the family that drives nine hours anyway comes home tired, poorer, and no faster — with a rider who missed two local rounds to finish mid-pack somewhere unfamiliar. Travel is a tool. Here is how to tell when it is the right one.

![A white truck driving on a road through mountains](/images/race-dad/travelling-out-of-province/travelling-out-of-province-01.jpg)

## When travel pays off

Three honest reasons to leave your home rounds. Level: a grid that is faster than your rider's usual competition is worth a tank of fuel twice a season, because riders improve against the pace in front of them, not the pace behind. Exposure: events where the people who matter to a developing career — series officials, teams, coaches — actually are, the way our Guangzhou trip put our rider in front of a whole different world. Variety: new tracks break the cruise control of a home circuit; a rider who has only ever turned left at Shannonville learns things at an unfamiliar venue that no amount of local laps teaches. If a trip serves none of those three, it is a vacation with bikes, and should be priced as one.

## When it does not

The honest counterweights. Budget: travel is the line item that quietly eats seasons — our budgeting post makes it the category that scales with the calendar you sign, and the true-cost post is the paddock proof. Fatigue: a nine-hour tow followed by a two-day round is not the same sporting event as a ninety-minute drive, and younger riders pay the fatigue tax at double rates. Development: racing unfamiliar grids every second weekend can cannibalize the coaching and track time at home that actually builds speed. The trips that pay are additions to a season, not replacements for it.

## Planning it honestly

The mechanics of a travel round deserve respect. Paperwork: licences and memberships that cross provincial or national lines sometimes need extra steps and extra lead time — check the rulebook of the series you are visiting, not your own, and check it early. Logistics: accommodations near regional tracks fill on race weekends months ahead; the family that books late sleeps far and drives early. And budget the trip as its own line — fuel, rooms, meals, entries, the extra tire you will want on an unfamiliar surface — before committing, so the decision is made in the living room rather than regretted at the pump.

## The right number

Every family finds its own count, but the shape we landed on: most of the season at home, a couple of marquee trips that serve level, exposure, or variety — and a firm no to the ones that serve only the story. Go far for the right reasons, and the miles come home with you in the rider. Go far for the wrong ones, and you mostly bought windshield time. Worth it, sometimes. The skill is knowing which times.
