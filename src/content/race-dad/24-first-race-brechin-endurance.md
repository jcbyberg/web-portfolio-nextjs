![Riders stretching together in the paddock before the endurance race at Brechin](/images/race-dad/first-race-brechin-endurance/riders-stretching-paddock-brechin.jpg)

Most families ease into racing. You do a school, then a practice day, then maybe a sprint round where the whole thing is over in twelve laps and you can pretend it didn't count.

We started with an endurance race.

Brechin Motorsport Park — the karting circuit at Gamebridge, north of Orillia — hosts a minimoto endurance round in the fall and another in the spring. The fall one was the first event we ever entered. Not a school day. An actual race, with a grid, a clock, and hours to fill.

The photo above is the bit nobody puts in the highlight reel: a dozen riders in full leathers doing a group stretch on the pavement before the start, arms over their heads, in among the folding chairs and a parked go-kart. It looks faintly ridiculous and it is completely serious. They are about to be out there for hours. Watching your own child in that group, for the first time, does something to your stomach that no amount of practice-day cone drills prepares you for.

![A small four-stroke coming through the infield at Brechin](/images/race-dad/first-race-brechin-endurance/four-stroke-infield-brechin.jpg)

Then the flag drops and the whole thing changes character. Sprint racing is a sprint. Endurance is arithmetic. Fuel. Tires. Rider changes. Who is fading and who is not. The pace that looks heroic in lap four is the pace that puts you in the grass in lap forty, and the kids who work that out early are the ones still circulating cleanly at the end.

The format the series runs puts newer riders and experienced teams in separate heats, which matters enormously the first time out. Nobody is asking a first-year kid to hold a line while a national-level rider comes past. They get to learn the thing without also being an obstacle.

![Two riders working through the back section as the light goes long](/images/race-dad/first-race-brechin-endurance/riders-back-section-evening-light.jpg)

What I did not expect was how much of the day belongs to the parents. Endurance means a pit board, a stopwatch, a plan for who goes out when, and a folding chair you never sit in. By mid-afternoon you know every family on your row by name, because you have all been standing in the same patch of pavement for hours watching for the same helmet to come round.

I have no lap chart from that day and no result worth quoting. Grassroots minimoto does not always publish one, and I am not going to invent numbers to make the story tidier. What I have is this: the first race we ever entered was an endurance race, the kids finished it, and by the drive home the question was not whether we were doing this again but when.

We have done two of these now — the fall round, and the spring one that follows it. That first one is the reason for all of it: the schools, the [MiniSBK](https://roadracecanada.ca/) season, the national rounds, the trailer, the whole thing.
