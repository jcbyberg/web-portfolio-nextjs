Minimoto racing punishes vibes-based maintenance. The bikes are small, simple, and utterly honest: keep the calendar and they run forever; wing it and the crankcase gods collect. This is the calendar itself — the service schedule mindset that beats them.

![A title card reading "Checklists win seasons." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/maintaining-a-minimoto/maintaining-a-minimoto-01.png)

## After every track day

The post-session half hour, in the paddock before the bike goes in the trailer. Clean it — actually clean it, because a clean bike shows its leaks, cracks, and worn parts, and a filthy one hides them all. Then the walk-around with eyes: spokes and rim integrity after a day of kerbs, chain tension and lubrication, cables moving freely, bolts that vibrate for a living (footpegs, levers, bodywork, axle nuts) checked with a wrench, not a glance. Tires inspected for cuts and wear, pressures set to storage baseline, fuel treated or drained for the drive home. Lever and pedal positions still where the rider's hands and feet expect. The point of the ritual is not the fifteen tasks; it is that the bike arrives home telling you what it needs before next time.

## Weekly during the season

The deeper check between track days, an hour with the manual: oil level and condition (top up or change if a weekend intervened), air filter cleaned and re-oiled, brake pads eyeballed for wear, brake fluid level, chain beyond tension — actual wear measurement, the stretch that says replace, not adjust. Two-stroke fleet items: plug color read, premix stock checked, power valve on its schedule. Everything electrical that only fails at the track — kill switch, transponder mount, connections — gets exercised now, because "it worked last time" is not a test.

## The off-season teardown

The thankless job that pays for the season. Every winter: full service of the things you raced all year without touching — fork oil, brake fluid, wheel bearings swung and spun for notchiness, steering head bearings loaded and checked, swingarm and linkage play measured, carb stripped and cleaned, top-end on a two-stroke inspected at minimum. Fasteners torqued to spec, chassis bolts out and greased, wiring loom looked at properly. This is also when you inventory the spares bin against what actually broke last year, and when the "while I'm in there" work gets done — because in-season, "while I'm in there" is how weekends die.

## The mindset

The calendar beats the anecdote because racing maintenance is boring on purpose: the point of the schedule is that nothing interesting ever happens. A failing that announces itself at the track already cost a session; the calendar catches it in the garage, where time is cheap. Keep the checklists physical — laminated, on the trailer wall, ticked with a marker — because the checklist you cannot see is the checklist you skip. Distinct from the crash-repair wrenching our track-side post covers: that is surgery after an incident, this is the vaccinations. Checklists win seasons. The crankcase gods only collect from the improvised.
