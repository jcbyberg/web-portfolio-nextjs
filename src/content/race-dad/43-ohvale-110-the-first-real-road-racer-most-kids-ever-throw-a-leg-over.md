There is a moment in every young rider's racing life when the pit bike era ends and a real road racer begins. For a lot of kids in our world, that machine is the Ohvale 110 — the first bike with a proper gearbox, real brakes, and race bodywork at kid scale. Here is what it is and why it matters.

![A title card reading "Small bike, real racing." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/ohvale-110-first-road-racer/ohvale-110-first-road-racer-01.png)

## What the 110 actually is

An Ohvale GP-0-class 110 is a miniature road racer built as a race bike first: full fairing, proper front brake with real bite, a sequential gearbox the rider has to operate, and geometry that rewards actual road-racing technique — braking markers, lean, throttle control. It is not a pit bike with bodywork. The distinction matters because everything a 110 teaches is transferable: the riding position, the corner entry, the discipline of a gearbox are the same skills the 160, the 190, and eventually full-size racing demand. Our GP-0 coverage goes deeper on the machine itself; this is the case for it as the first real road racer.

## Who it is for

The 110 is the step after the 50cc and strada-style classes — riders who can already ride a basic mini reliably, control speed, and — most importantly — listen in a group. The gearbox is the gate: a rider who cannot yet think ahead three seconds will find a clutch and shift pattern overwhelming on a race track. The readiness signs are covered in our progression post, but the short version is braking with intent, awareness of other riders, and coachability under pressure. When those are in place, the 110 is the most fun a young rider can legally have on two wheels.

## What it teaches that a pit bike cannot

A clutch. Manual clutch control is the single biggest technical gift the 110 gives a young rider — starts, corner exits, and the whole feel-based relationship with a drivetrain that road racing runs on. Real braking: a front brake that can stop the bike properly teaches brake discipline, which is the foundation of lap time at every level after. And racecraft in a real class: the 110s race hard, close, and fair, and the lessons — holding a line, planning a pass a corner in advance, losing a position without losing the plot — are exactly the ones that follow a rider up every class.

## In the family fleet

Practically: the 110 is light enough for parent transport without a gym membership, simple enough that home maintenance is realistic — our wrenching posts are mostly written around these bikes — and cheap enough to run that track time is not rationed. Spares are available, the class structure at MiniSBK and MotoMini events gives it a real grid, and the resale market is healthy because the next family always needs one. It is the bike where the kid stops being a passenger on the idea of racing and becomes a racer with a tool that rewards them.

The first time you watch your rider click down two gears into a corner and out-brake someone on a real race motorcycle, you will understand why the paddock calls this the class where it starts. The 110 is small. What it begins is not.
