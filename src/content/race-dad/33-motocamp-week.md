In the middle of the road racing season, Cohen put the leathers away for a week and went to Motocamp.

![A rider in the air over a jump on a dirt track](/images/race-dad/motocamp-week/dirt-bike-jump-motocamp.jpg)

Dirt is not what he does. His year is tarmac — the 190, the go-kart circuits, tire warmers, the same corner approached the same way until it stops requiring thought. A week in the dirt is the opposite of that on nearly every axis. The surface moves. The bike moves. Nothing is repeatable, and the thing you are being asked to get good at is not precision but recovery.

I have opinions about why that is useful, and I want to be careful to label them as opinions, because I was not there and I am reporting a week I did not see. What I know for certain is that he came back happy and the bike came back running, barely.

The case for sending a road racer to the dirt is that road racing punishes you for losing the front and the dirt teaches you what losing the front feels like early enough to do something about it. On tarmac a slide is an event. In the dirt a slide is Tuesday. Riders who came up through dirt seem to have a different relationship with the bike moving underneath them — less alarm, more information — and you can watch that difference in how quickly somebody gets back on the throttle after a moment.

Whether one week does that, I genuinely do not know. It might just have been a good week outside in July with people his own age, which would be reason enough on its own. There is a version of this where every activity has to be justified as development, and that version is exhausting for everyone and especially for the kid.

What I will say is that he asked to go, which is the part that matters most to me. Nobody suggested a dirt week as a training intervention. He wanted to ride something different for a while, and the family calendar had a hole in it, and the answer was yes.

The bike making it to the end — barely — is its own small piece of education, and one he will get more of. Everything mechanical on a racing weekend is a countdown you are trying to outlast.
