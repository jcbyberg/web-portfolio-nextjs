Eleven seconds of video, and not much appears to happen in it. A kid on a borrowed green Kawasaki, a coach walking away across the pavement, a few cones knocked flat. If you did not know what you were looking at you would scroll straight past it.

What you are looking at is Abel working out a clutch for the first time.

Up to that point everything he had ridden did the thinking for him. You turn the throttle, the bike goes. You shut it, the bike slows. There is nothing to coordinate and nothing to get wrong except the part where you point it. A clutch changes the deal entirely. Suddenly there is a lever that does not stop the bike, it *disconnects* it, and the whole business of pulling away depends on a band of travel maybe a centimetre wide that nobody can point to and nobody can explain in words that help.

The friction zone. Every rider finds it the same way, which is badly, over and over, until they don't.

![Abel on the school's Kawasaki, Super Sonic sticker on the shroud, with another helmeted rider alongside](/images/race-dad/first-clutch/abel-first-clutch-super-sonic-kawasaki.jpg)

He stalled it. Then he stalled it again. Then he let it out too fast and the bike jumped forward and stopped dead, which is the version that rattles a kid, because it feels like the motorcycle did something to him rather than the other way around. Nobody rushed him. That is the thing about the [Super Sonic](https://roadracecanada.ca/) days — there is no clock running and nothing to win in a parking lot, so a ten-year-old is allowed to be bad at something in public for as long as it takes.

Then he wasn't. The revs came up, the lever came out to meet them, and he rolled away without a lurch and without looking down at his hand. Once that happens it does not really un-happen.

I did not get the good version on camera. What I have is the working-it-out version, which is honestly the more useful one. In a year he will not remember that this was hard. He will get on a geared bike, pull away, and have no idea that there was ever a version of him who could not.

From the fence it looked like nothing. It was the whole afternoon.
