Walk any minimoto paddock at the end of a weekend and you can reconstruct the season from what people carried. The family with the organized bin rode Sunday. The family that packed like a catalogue flew home Saturday afternoon waiting for a courier. The spares you need are not a matter of budget — they are a matter of probability times catastrophe, and you can do that math by watching.

![A title card reading "Pack for the likely, not the catalogue." for Race Dad](/images/race-dad/spare-parts-bin/spare-parts-bin-01.png)

## The matrix: probability times catastrophe

Every part earns its place in the bin by two numbers. How likely is it to break or wear out on a race weekend — and when it does, is the race over? A bent lever is likely and cheap: carry two. A cracked case is catastrophic and rare: that is what insurance and a season of careful bike prep are for. The bin is built from the top-left of that matrix — the likely-and-race-ending things — and it is smaller than you think. Crash-repair time at the track is twenty minutes, our crash-repair post walks the real version of this; everything in the bin is what makes those twenty minutes possible.

## What actually breaks

Levers, first and always — front and rear brake, clutch, because every paddock fall eats one. Handlebars, because the same fall that eats a lever eats the bar it is bolted to. Pegs and their pins. Control cables: throttle and clutch, pre-routed and learned, because a frayed cable found at tech is a morning gone. Chain master links, one or two, and the little chain-breaking tool you hope to never use. Spark plugs. Tubes for the minis that run them. Fuses. And the fastener kit — the bolts that hold on the number plate are the bolts that hold on the weekend. Add clutch plates for a mini ridden hard, and you have covered most of what actually ends race days.

## Build it from the paddock, not the catalogue

Here is the method that works better than any shopping list: keep a blank page in the toolbox all season, and write down every part that broke, bent, or got borrowed at every round — yours and anyone you helped. Two seasons of that page is your bin, tuned to your bike, your class, and your rider's particular talent for finding walls. The parts catalogue wants to sell you the whole matrix; the paddock will tell you which corner of it you live in. Bigger teams carry more because they run more bikes, not because the math is different.

## How to carry it

One bin, not a mystery — a tackle box or a labeled drawer set where every part has a place, and a laminated list inside the lid so packing takes ten minutes and a glance. Restock the night you get home, while the memory of what got used is fresh. And keep the tools that fit the parts with the parts. The bin's whole job is to turn a crash from a drive home into a story at the podium — pack for the likely, not the catalogue, and you will ride Sunday more often than the family that packed three times as much.
