Lombardy Raceway sits north of Kingston, about three hours east of us, which makes it the one on the calendar that is a trip rather than a drive. We have been twice.

The first was in early October 2025, at the end of a season, with the leaves going over and the mornings cold enough that nobody wanted to be the first out. There is no published result from that weekend that I can point you at — a lot of grassroots minimoto simply is not written up anywhere — and I am not going to reconstruct one from memory to make this read better. What I remember is the light. Low autumn sun straight down the back section, and a kid on a red minibike who did not want to come in.

![The MotoMini grid forming up at Lombardy for round two](/images/race-dad/lombardy-two-visits/motomini-grid-lombardy-round-two.jpg)

The second trip was round two of the 2026 [FIM MotoMini Canada](https://roadracecanada.ca/) season, on July 4. Completely different weather, completely different stakes.

Jäger Stockill took both 190 races — the second one by more than twenty-five seconds, which at Lombardy is most of a lap. In race two Cohen came home fourth in the class, in his rookie season at that level, on a circuit he had seen for the first time the day before.

![The MotoMini Canada podium at Lombardy after round two](/images/race-dad/lombardy-two-visits/motomini-canada-podium-lombardy.jpg)

Lombardy is a short track — well under a kilometre — and it does not let you hide. There is no long straight to recover a bad exit on. If you carry the corner badly, you carry it badly all the way to the next one, and the rider ahead simply leaves. That is a hard lesson for a kid who has learned to ride somewhere with more room, and it is the reason the trip is worth making twice a year.

The drive home from Lombardy is the long one. Three hours, a trailer, and three kids asleep in the back with their helmet bags as pillows. Both times.
