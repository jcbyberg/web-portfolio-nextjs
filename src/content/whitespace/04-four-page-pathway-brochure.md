The largest print piece in the set: a four-page 17 × 11 brochure, laid out with proper bleed lines for the printer.

The content is *The Canadian Path to Circuit Racing: From Playground to Podium*, and the reason it needed four pages instead of one is that it is a story with five chapters that only work in order.

**Starting line.** Super Sonic Road Race School, age five and up. Throttle control, body position, braking, the basics of racecraft.

**First competition.** MiniSBK — grassroots, family-oriented, real wheel-to-wheel racing.

**The national stage.** FIM MotoMini Canada, age ten and up. A national championship run to international Grand Prix format.

**The world.** Finish first or second in that championship and you are on a plane to the FIM MotoMini World Finals.

**The bridge to big bikes.** The Super Sonic Bridge Program, moving graduates up onto full-size Yamaha YZF-R3s.

The design job here is sequence. A parent picking this up does not want a feature list — they want to see that there is a road, that their kid can get on it at five years old, and that it goes somewhere real. So the layout moves left to right like a timeline, each stage sitting visually a step above the last, with ages and entry points marked at every gate.

Everything is built in editable fields. Prices and dates change every season. The layout shouldn't have to.
