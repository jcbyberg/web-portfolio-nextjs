I found something while researching an organization recently and I keep thinking about it.

At the end of every session they run, they email each person a gallery of photos and video from their day. Every session. Every person. They've done it for years.

None of it has ever gone on their social media. Not one photo.

They've got thousands of images sitting in a folder, produced automatically just by doing their job. And their social media is quiet, because — their words — they don't have time to make content.

They're not unusual. This is normal for a small organization. Most charities reading this have the same folder.

## The problem isn't the camera

When a group says it can't do social media, it almost always blames gear. We don't have a photographer. We don't have a videographer. We don't have the equipment.

Then you go looking and find a volunteer who photographs every event on a decent phone. Someone's brother-in-law shot the fundraiser. There's a hard drive with four years of programs on it. The AGM was recorded. Somebody's been quietly building an archive nobody asked for.

The gear was never the problem. It's three things nobody's been put in charge of:

**Nobody knows the photos exist.** They're on a personal drive, in an email thread, on a phone that's never been backed up. The organization doesn't have them. A person who volunteers there has them.

**Nobody knows what's allowed.** Someone photographed forty people. Which ones signed something? Was it a media release or just a sign-in sheet? With kids this stops everything cold, and it should. But the honest answer is usually "we don't know," and that works out the same as "no."

**Nobody can approve a post fast.** The photo's fine, the caption's written, and it needs a yes from a director who checks email on Sundays. Three weeks later it's not news anymore and it quietly dies.

Fix those three and most of the problem goes away. None of them costs money.

## Post the boring half

When groups do post, they post the polished bit. The cheque handover. The ribbon. The group photo at the end with everyone smiling.

The stuff that actually works is the other half of the day. Setup at 6:30am in the dark. Someone laying out gear in rows. The van getting loaded. The volunteer briefing in a car park. The cleanup nobody photographs because everyone's tired by then.

Two reasons, and neither is about looking good.

First, nobody else can copy it. Anyone can post a photo of a cheque. Nobody else has your 6:30am.

Second, it answers the question people are too polite to ask. A parent deciding whether to send their kid, or a donor deciding whether you're serious, is really asking: *are these people organised?* A fundraising total doesn't answer that. Forty labelled bins at dawn does.

## Three questions this week

Don't start by buying a camera. Start by asking:

1. **Who's already taking photos, and where do they live right now?** Name the person. Get access to the folder. That's the first step and usually the only hard one.
2. **What did people actually sign, and does it cover posting online?** Read the form. If it doesn't cover it, write one that does and use it from now on. Treat everything before that as unusable, because it is.
3. **Who can say yes to a post within a day?** One name. Someone who'll actually look. Without this, everything else just sits in a queue.

That's it. No equipment, no agency, no budget. Three questions, and most groups can answer all three in an afternoon.

The content already exists. It's been piling up for years while nobody looked. The work isn't making more. It's finding what you've got, checking you're allowed to use it, and clearing a path so it can go out while it still matters.
