A furnace quits on a Tuesday night in February. The homeowner does not open a laptop. They pick up the phone that is already in their hand, search for someone local, and start tapping results. Whoever makes the next thirty seconds easy gets the job. Everyone else gets looked at and closed.

That is the whole contest for a local trade, and it is usually lost on details nobody would call a design problem. The site is not ugly. The information is all there. It is just a hair out of line, the way a press sheet goes soft when one plate drifts.

![Press plate: three registration targets labelled tap-to-call, service area and page weight, the third one printed out of register](/images/whitespace/three-website-mistakes-local-trades/out-of-register.png)

## One: nothing on the page dials your phone

Plenty of trade sites list a phone number as plain text, or hide it behind a contact form. On a desktop that is fine. On a phone it means pinch, zoom, select the digits without catching the fax line, copy, switch apps, paste. Every one of those steps is a chance to give up and go back to the search results, where four competitors are waiting.

The fix is not a redesign. It is a real tap-to-call link, in the header, on every page, sized like it matters. A customer with a flooded basement should be able to reach you with one thumb without reading anything first.

## Two: your service area is left to the reader's imagination

"Serving the Greater Toronto Area" tells a homeowner in Whitby almost nothing. It certainly does not tell them whether you will drive out on a Sunday.

If the towns you actually work in are not written on the page, neither a customer nor a search engine can confirm you cover them. Name them. Oshawa, Whitby, Ajax, Courtice, Bowmanville, whatever the real list is, in ordinary words on a page a person can read. This is the cheapest edit on this list and it is the one most often skipped, because it feels too obvious to bother with.

## Three: the site is heaviest where it matters least

The full-screen autoplay video, the image slider nobody swipes, the stack of plugins added one emergency at a time. On office wifi none of it registers. In a driveway on a phone with two bars, that weight is time, and time is the customer deciding you are not worth the wait.

Cut the decoration and keep the proof. Photos of your own finished work do more for a trade business than any animation, and they load in a fraction of the time.

## What a trade website actually has to do

A homeowner in a bad moment wants three things, in this order: proof you are local, proof you are competent, and an obvious way to call you right now. Everything else on the page is optional, and quite a lot of it is in the way.

None of the three mistakes above is a failure of taste. They are registration errors. The parts are all on the sheet, one of them sits slightly off, and the job goes to whoever's page landed square.
